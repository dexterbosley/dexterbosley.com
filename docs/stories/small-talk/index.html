Small Talk

JAN 24, 2026
< BACK

I.

It was Thursday. The boys watched traffic grind uptown.

II.

Steve tapped his black shoes together.

“Don’t,” Elliott said.

“What?”

“You’ll scuff ’em.”

Steve held his feet apart. “I’m hungry.”

“Those sandwiches sucked.”

“Like the soccer ones.”

“I’ll make you something tonight,” Elliott said. “There’s tortellini.”

Steve smiled. “Where’s Mom?”

“Still inside, I think.”

Steve turned toward the oak doors. “She never liked coming here.”

“Remember that thing she sent us? About how kids should sleep in?”

“She sent it twice.”

“She sends everything twice.”

A crash. On the sidewalk to their right, water and glass, cut flowers, white and pink, a man, his cheek pressed into pavement, above, a girl and a balcony rail. A scream.

“Did she drop that?”

“Had to… I don’t… Stevey, he’s not moving,” Elliott whispered.

Someone ran across the street. Faces followed. A delivery worker shook the man and a different man stabbed at his phone. There were sirens and flashes and uniforms.

Steve watched the concrete stain and Elliott sank into the shade and the girl and her elbow slipped inside.

III.

A man approached, not the priest. “It’s time to carry your sister outside.”

Steve got up and rubbed his eyes. He waited for his brother.
