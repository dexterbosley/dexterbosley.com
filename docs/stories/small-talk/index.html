Small Talk

❧January 18, 2025 · Story · 2min

The brothers stood outside the hospice and talked and talked. But never about one thing. They talked about sports and weather and work. They talked about people they had lost touch with and what they thought they were up to. They talked about everything but the woman they had just been told would soon become another person they had lost touch with.

Steve went silent as Elliott described how his office would soon transition to a hybrid model. A building caught his eye—directly across the street, maybe ten stories high. And then. A window on the fifth floor burst in a violent shower of glass. Shards rained down on a sidewalk flower stand. Steve stared, stuck still, as a man walked by, unaware. Jagged glass danced. Free: cut flowers, white and pink. A man and his cheek were pressed into the pavement. Steve looked up. Stuck against a balcony rail was a girl, shaking underneath loose sweats. “Did she… drop that?”

“Had to… look at all that glass, and… I don’t… Steve, he’s not moving,” Elliott whispered.

A woman ran across the street toward the scene. More faces followed. A delivery worker shook the man; another stabbed at his phone. Steve noticed the girl again: curled on the curb. She stared through the crowd.

Later, there were sirens and flashes and uniforms until: an empty street. Elliott sank into the shade to rest. Steve focused on traffic. The girl slipped upstairs.

Someone else approached. He stopped. “I… we’re ready. It’s time to carry your mom outside.” Steve got up first and rubbed his eyes to see now open doors. It would not be until much later that both men could claim to see clearly together.
