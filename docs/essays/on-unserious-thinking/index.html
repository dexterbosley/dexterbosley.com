On Unserious Thinking

OCT 9, 2026

I.

Expecting b2c agents to take over commerce feels unserious. Businesses will protect their margins with their own agents or block outside ones. Consider ecommerce, all the rage these days with agents like Muse and Instinct.

II.

On its Q3 2026 earnings call Meta reported over 10m Muse setups in the US and Canada with about 25% active daily. Over a quarter of weekly users used it for shopping with average purchases above $200, including travel.

Let’s assume:

  • Twice as many weekly users as daily users: 2.5m daily → 5m weekly.
  • A quarter of those weekly users buy once a month at $200: $3bn in annual purchases.
  • A 5% total take: roughly 3% for card processing including interchange plus 2% for the agent → $150m in annual gross fees.

III.

In year three (so 2029-ish):

  • Bear, roughly pre-IPO Duolingo pace (20% annual growth in monthly users): 10m weekly users × 25% buying monthly → $300m annual gross fees.
  • Base, roughly early Snapchat pace (daily users roughly doubling in a year): 50m weekly users × 25% buying monthly → $1.5bn annual gross fees.
  • Bull, roughly ChatGPT pace (weekly users grew from 200m to nearly 700m in a year): 200m weekly users × 50% buying twice monthly → $24bn annual gross fees.

By year three Muse would need $480bn in annual purchases (about 5× DoorDash’s 2025 order volume) or $4,800 per shopper. At a 5% take (roughly 3% for Stripe’s full processing fee + 2% convenience fee) and zero assumed costs:

IV.

Self-discovery can subsidize the price paid by the customer through advertising. I expect businesses to use agents to improve algorithmically targeted performance ads and keep the eyeballs viewing them.

Consider Amazon’s advertising business and Meta’s own ad business. These totals include brand and video ads too:

Revenue / increase
Muse bull case, year three (5% total take) $24bn
Amazon ad revenue increase, 2025 vs. 2024 $12.4bn
Amazon total ad revenue, 2025 $68.6bn
Meta ad revenue increase, 2025 vs. 2024 $35.5bn
Meta total ad revenue, 2025 $196.2bn

A 12.2% lift in Meta’s ad revenue would add about $24bn a year, matching Muse’s bull case. If advertiser spending rises proportionally with conversion, that could mean going from a 2% conversion rate to about 2.24%. Meta probably gets more from its constrained inference capacity by improving ad targeting than by putting it into Muse.

V.

Digital performance advertising may be the best business model ever. Amazon has already blocked Muse and sued to stop Perplexity’s Comet from shopping on its site. It handles an estimated 39.7% of US retail ecommerce sales in 2026.

Consumer productivity agents feel like a shortsighted grasp at use cases to me. Travel makes sense when people already have a destination and dates and want help booking. Beyond that I struggle to see the case for an independent agent operating on top of the existing ecommerce ecosystem.

Amazon has the same incentive as Meta: use inference to improve its own agents and ad engine and keep shoppers in its walled garden. The businesses independent agents need access to have more reason to protect that model than to help outsiders bypass it.
