The lamp had been in the window for as long as anyone on the block could remember. It was brass, or something like brass, with a shade the color of old paper.

---

Margaret turned it on every evening at six and turned it off when she went to bed. She had done this since her husband died, which was eleven years ago, or maybe twelve. She could not remember the exact year anymore, only the season. It was fall.

The neighbors noticed the lamp the way people notice church bells — only when they stopped. One Tuesday the lamp did not come on. Mrs. Huang across the street saw it first and mentioned it to her husband, who said nothing.

---

By Thursday a small group had gathered on the sidewalk. They looked up at the dark window and spoke in low voices. Someone suggested knocking. No one knocked.

On Friday the lamp came on again at six. The group dispersed. Margaret, who had been visiting her sister in New Jersey, never learned that anyone had noticed.
