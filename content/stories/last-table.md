The restaurant was closing for good on Sunday. Roberto had owned it for thirty-one years and his knees had owned it for about twenty-five.

---

"How many covers tonight?" asked his daughter, who did the books and worried about the things Roberto refused to worry about.

"Forty-two. We could do sixty if we pushed the tables together."

"We're not pushing the tables together."

"I know."

The last seating was at nine. Roberto had considered making it earlier but nine felt right. People should eat late on the last night. They should linger. They should order dessert even if they never order dessert.

---

At ten forty-five, the dining room was still full. No one had asked for a check. The kitchen had run out of the risotto at nine-thirty, then the branzino at ten. Roberto was now cooking with whatever was left, which was mostly pasta and good olive oil and the parmesan he'd been saving for no reason he could name.

His daughter found him in the kitchen at midnight, sitting on an overturned crate, eating spaghetti from the pot with a wooden spoon. He was crying, but only a little, and only because the spaghetti was very good.
