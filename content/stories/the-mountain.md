They had been climbing for four hours when Paul stopped and said he couldn't feel his hands.

---

"Wiggle your fingers," said Ray, who was ahead by ten feet and did not turn around.

"I'm wiggling them."

"Can you feel them wiggling?"

Paul looked at his gloves. His fingers were moving inside them but the sensation was remote, like watching someone else's hands. "Sort of."

Ray finally turned. His face was red from the wind and his goggles were pushed up on his forehead. He looked like a man who had just been told something he already knew.

"We should go down," Ray said.

"We're almost there."

"We're not almost there. We're almost at the ridge. The summit is another two hours past the ridge and the weather is turning."

---

Paul looked up. The sky was the same gray it had been all morning — a flat, indifferent gray that could mean anything. He wanted to argue but his hands were making the argument for him.

They turned around at eleven-fifteen. They reached the car at three. Paul's hands were fine by dinner.

He never went back to that mountain, though he talked about it for years.
