In most organizations, the person who speaks the loudest in a meeting is presumed to have the best ideas. This is almost never true. Speaking rewards confidence, charisma, and quick thinking. Writing rewards clarity, depth, and precision.

---

Amazon's memo culture is instructive. Before any meeting of consequence, attendees read a six-page narrative memo in silence. The memo format forces the author to think through their argument completely — no bullet points to hide behind, no slides to distract from weak reasoning.

The result is that ideas compete on merit rather than on the presentation skills of their advocates. A quiet engineer with a well-reasoned memo outperforms a charismatic executive with a vague slide deck.

---

I think every company would benefit from writing more and meeting less. Not because meetings are bad, but because writing forces a level of rigor that speaking does not. You cannot write a clear sentence about a confused idea. The act of writing reveals the confusion.
