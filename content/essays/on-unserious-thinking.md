## **I.**

Much ado about whether consumers want shopping agents. I'm skeptical. The businesses that matter at scale have little incentive to support an independent agent shopping on the consumer's behalf. They will protect their margins with their own agents or block outside ones.

## **II.**

On its [Q3 2026 earnings call](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/meta/transcripts/718643-q3-2026/) Meta reported [over 10m Muse setups in the US and Canada with about 25% active daily](https://apnews.com/article/13ecd6c459e39a3602f302bcc2412fe9). Over a quarter of weekly users used it for shopping with average purchases above $200, including travel.

To model three-year business outcomes, let's assume:

- Twice as many weekly users as daily users: **2.5m daily → 5m weekly**.
- A quarter of those weekly users buy once a month at $200: **$3bn in annual purchases**.
- A 5% take for Muse: equivalent to [Stripe's entire processing charge](https://stripe.com/pricing) of roughly 3% plus a 2% convenience fee → **$150m in annual gross fees**.

All lines start at 5m weekly users. ChatGPT grew from [200m weekly users in August 2024](https://www.reuters.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/openai-says-chatgpts-weekly-users-have-grown-200-million-2024-08-29/) to [nearly 700m in August 2025](https://www.reuters.com/business/openai-eyes-500-billion-valuation-potential-employee-share-sale-source-says-2025-08-06/), roughly 3.5× in a year. Sustaining that pace for three years from 5m gives **214m weekly users**. That's the dashed reference line. The bull case reaches **twice that year-three user count: 429m**. Bear and base reach 10m and 50m.

The chart models Muse users, not actual ChatGPT users. All curves compound smoothly from the same starting point; the bull case reaches twice the reference at year three. Figures and annual growth rates are rounded.

## **III.**

In year three (so 2029-ish):

- **Bear: 5m → 10m weekly users**, approximately **26% annual growth**. At 25% buying once a month, $200 per purchase and a 5% take: **$300m annual gross fees**.
- **Base: 5m → 50m weekly users**, approximately **115% annual growth**. At 25% buying once a month, $200 per purchase and a 5% take: **$1.5bn annual gross fees**.
- **Bull (twice the ChatGPT-pace year-three users): 5m → 429m weekly users**, approximately **341% annual growth**. At 50% buying twice a month, $200 per purchase and a 5% take: **$51.5bn annual gross fees**.

The bull case also doubles the share of users who shop and their purchase frequency relative to bear and base. By year three that means roughly 214m shoppers spending $4,800 each annually through Muse: **$1.03tn in gross merchandise value (GMV)**.

That's about 10× [DoorDash's $102bn in 2025 marketplace gross order value](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000179278926000013/dash-20251231.htm). The $4,800 per shopper also exceeds the [$4,280 average annual US ecommerce spend per shopper in Landmark Global's 2026 factsheet](https://landmarkglobal.com/uploads/26-CFS/Factssheet-US-2026.pdf).

At a 5% take (Stripe's entire processing charge plus a 2% convenience fee) and zero assumed costs:

| Annual figures | Muse bull case, year three | [Meta Family of Apps, 2025 actuals](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Revenue | $51.5bn | $198.8bn |
| Operating profit | $51.5bn | $102.5bn |
| Operating margin | 100% assumed | 51.6% actual |

## **IV.**

Now consider [Amazon's advertising business](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm) and [Meta's own ad business](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm):

| | Revenue / increase |
| --- | ---: |
| Muse bull case, year three (5% total take) | $51.5bn |
| Amazon ad revenue increase, 2025 vs. 2024 | $12.4bn |
| Amazon total ad revenue, 2025 | $68.6bn |
| Meta ad revenue increase, 2025 vs. 2024 | $35.5bn |
| Meta total ad revenue, 2025 | $196.2bn |

A 26.2% increase on Meta's 2025 ad revenue would bring in $51.5bn a year, matching Muse's most optimistic forecast. In 2025, Meta actually grew ad revenue by [22.1%, or $35.5bn](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm). Matching Muse would require growth 4.1 percentage points faster on that larger revenue base. Improving ad targeting may be a better use of its scarce computing capacity than building Muse.

Amazon has a similar investment choice. It can put compute into agents that improve product discovery and ad targeting within its existing store. Advertising revenue can also fund lower prices. If Amazon devoted a quarter of that revenue to discounts after ad costs, it could cut prices by roughly 2%. An upstart must fund its computing costs and customer acquisition from the fees shoppers will pay for convenience.

## **V.**

Travel is a legitimate consumer use case. Beyond that, b2c shopping agents bet on a consumer preference for productivity that feels absurd when the best consumer product of all time is an infinite feed of nonsense.

The businesses that matter at scale have little reason to support an independent agent serving the shopper. And the promised mom-and-pop insurgency need only look at [Amazon to see who ends up in control](https://www.ft.com/content/64f01bfe-39b9-49f6-86eb-be96374539cf).

In 2026, we're still in search of a broader consumer case.
