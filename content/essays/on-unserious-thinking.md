## **I.**

Businesses will protect their margins with their own agents or block outside ones. Consider ecommerce, all the rage these days with agents like Muse and Instinct.

## **II.**

On its [Q3 2026 earnings call](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/meta/transcripts/718643-q3-2026/) Meta reported over 10m Muse setups in the US and Canada with about 25% active daily. Over a quarter of weekly users used it for shopping with average purchases above $200, including travel.

So let's assume:

- Twice as many weekly users as daily users: **2.5m daily → 5m weekly**.
- A quarter of those weekly users buy once a month at $200: **$3bn in annual purchases**.
- A 5% take for Muse: equivalent to [Stripe's entire processing charge](https://stripe.com/us/pricing) of roughly 3% plus a 2% convenience fee → **$150m in annual gross fees**.

[ChatGPT doubled weekly users in roughly nine months](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/openai-says-chatgpts-weekly-users-have-grown-to-200-million-3594410). The dashed line applies that pace to the same assumed 5m starting point:

![Three-year weekly-user growth from 5m: bear 10m, base 50m and bull 200m. ChatGPT's nine-month doubling pace extrapolated from the same starting point reaches 80m.](/images/on-unserious-thinking/muse-growth-cases.svg)

## **III.**

In year three (so 2029-ish):

- Bear, roughly [pre-IPO Duolingo](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828021014753/duolingo424b4.htm) pace (20% annual growth in monthly users): 10m weekly users × 25% buying monthly → **$300m annual gross fees**.
- Base, roughly [early Snapchat](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000119312517029199/d270216ds1.htm) pace (daily users roughly doubling in a year): 50m weekly users × 25% buying monthly → **$1.5bn annual gross fees**.
- Bull, roughly [ChatGPT](https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-new-era-of-work/) pace (weekly users grew from 200m to nearly 700m in a year): 200m weekly users × 50% buying twice monthly → **$24bn annual gross fees**.

The bull case assumes ChatGPT-like user growth plus twice the shopping rate and purchase frequency. By year three that means 100m shoppers spending $4,800 each annually through Muse: **$480bn in gross merchandise value (GMV)**.

That's about 5× [DoorDash's $102bn in 2025 marketplace gross order value](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000179278926000013/dash-20251231.htm). The $4,800 per shopper also exceeds the [$4,280 average annual US ecommerce spend per shopper in Landmark Global's 2026 factsheet](https://landmarkglobal.com/uploads/26-CFS/Factssheet-US-2026.pdf).

At a 5% take (Stripe's entire processing charge plus a 2% convenience fee) and zero assumed costs:

| Annual figures | Muse bull case, year three | [Meta Family of Apps, 2025 actuals](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Revenue | $24bn | $198.8bn |
| Operating profit | $24bn | $102.5bn |
| Operating margin | 100% assumed | 51.6% actual |

## **IV.**

Now consider [Amazon's advertising business](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm) and [Meta's own ad business](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm):

| | Revenue / increase |
| --- | ---: |
| Muse bull case, year three (5% total take) | $24bn |
| Amazon ad revenue increase, 2025 vs. 2024 | $12.4bn |
| Amazon total ad revenue, 2025 | $68.6bn |
| Meta ad revenue increase, 2025 vs. 2024 | $35.5bn |
| Meta total ad revenue, 2025 | $196.2bn |

A 12.2% lift in Meta's ad revenue would add about $24bn annually, matching Muse's bull case. If advertiser spending scales with conversion, moving from 2% to about 2.24% would suffice. Meta's constrained inference capacity likely earns more improving ad targeting than powering Muse. If Amazon put 25% of ad revenue toward discounts after ad costs, that would fund $17.2bn annually, roughly 2% of [its estimated $830bn in 2025 merchandise sales](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/amazon-gmv-surpassed-800-billion-in-2025). Matching that $4 subsidy on a $200 purchase would consume Muse's entire 2% convenience fee before inference costs.

## **V.**

b2c shopping agents bet on a consumer preference for productivity that feels absurd when the best consumer product of all time is an infinite feed of nonsense. Travel makes sense when people already have a destination and dates and want help booking. Beyond that, I struggle to see the case for an independent agent operating on top of the existing ecommerce ecosystem.
