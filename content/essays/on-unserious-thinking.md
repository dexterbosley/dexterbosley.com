## **I.**

Expecting b2c agents to take over commerce feels unserious. Businesses will protect their margins with their own agents or block outside ones.

## **II.**

Consider ecommerce, all the rage these days with agents like Muse and Instinct. On its [Q3 2026 earnings call](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/meta/transcripts/718643-q3-2026/) Meta reported over 10m Muse setups in the US and Canada with about 25% active daily. Over a quarter of weekly users used it for shopping with average purchases above $200, including travel.

Let's assume:

- Twice as many weekly users as daily users: **2.5m daily → 5m weekly**.
- A quarter of those weekly users buy once a month at $200: **$3bn in annual purchases**.
- A 5% total take: roughly 3% for [card processing including interchange](https://stripe.com/us/pricing) plus 2% for the agent → **$150m in annual gross fees**.

[ChatGPT doubled weekly users in roughly nine months](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/openai-says-chatgpts-weekly-users-have-grown-to-200-million-3594410). The dashed line applies that pace to the same assumed 5m starting point:

![Three-year weekly-user growth from 5m: bear 10m, base 50m and bull 200m. ChatGPT's nine-month doubling pace extrapolated from the same starting point reaches 80m.](/images/on-unserious-thinking/muse-growth-cases.svg)

## **III.**

In year three:

- Bear, roughly [pre-IPO Duolingo](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828021014753/duolingo424b4.htm) pace (20% annual growth in monthly users): 10m weekly users × 25% buying monthly → **$300m annual gross fees**.
- Base, roughly [early Snapchat](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000119312517029199/d270216ds1.htm) pace (daily users roughly doubling in a year): 50m weekly users × 25% buying monthly → **$1.5bn annual gross fees**.
- Bull, roughly [ChatGPT](https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-new-era-of-work/) pace (weekly users grew from 200m to nearly 700m in a year): 200m weekly users × 50% buying twice monthly → **$24bn annual gross fees**.

The bull case requires $480bn in annual purchases or $4,800 per shopper. At a 5% take (roughly 3% for Stripe's full processing fee + 2% convenience fee) and zero assumed costs:

| Annual figures | Muse bull case, year three | [Meta Family of Apps, 2025 actuals](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Revenue | $24bn | $198.8bn |
| Operating profit | $24bn | $102.5bn |
| Operating margin | 100% assumed | 51.6% actual |

## **IV.**

Self-discovery can subsidize the price paid by the customer through advertising. I expect businesses to use agents to improve algorithmically targeted performance ads and keep the eyeballs viewing them.

Consider [Amazon's advertising business](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm) and [Meta's own ad business](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm). These totals include brand and video ads too:

| | Revenue / increase |
| --- | ---: |
| Muse bull case, year three (5% total take) | $24bn |
| Amazon ad revenue increase, 2025 vs. 2024 | $12.4bn |
| Amazon total ad revenue, 2025 | $68.6bn |
| Meta ad revenue increase, 2025 vs. 2024 | $35.5bn |
| Meta total ad revenue, 2025 | $196.2bn |

A 12.2% lift in Meta's ad revenue would add about $24bn a year, matching Muse's bull case. If advertiser spending rises proportionally with conversion, that could mean going from a 2% conversion rate to about 2.24%. Meta probably gets more from its constrained inference capacity by improving ad targeting than by putting it into Muse.

## **V.**

Consumer productivity agents feel like a shortsighted grasp at use cases to me. Travel makes sense when people already have a destination and dates and want help booking. Beyond that I struggle to see the case for an independent agent operating on top of the existing ecommerce ecosystem.

Digital performance advertising may be the best business model ever. Amazon has already [blocked Muse](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/21/amazon-meta-muse-ai-agentic-shopping) and [sued to stop Perplexity's Comet from shopping on its site](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/perplexity-receives-legal-threat-from-amazon-over-agentic-ai-shopping-tool-4331628). It handles an estimated [39.7% of US retail ecommerce sales in 2026](https://www.emarketer.com/content/amazon-still-out-draws-its-three-closest-rivals).

Amazon has the same incentive as Meta: use inference to improve its own agents and ad engine and keep shoppers in its walled garden. The businesses independent agents need access to have more reason to protect that model than to help outsiders bypass it.
