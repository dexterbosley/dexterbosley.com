## **I.**

Much ado about whether shopping agents are the new paradigm. I'm skeptical. Even if consumer demand for agents materializes, the businesses that matter at scale have little incentive to support an independent agent shopping on the consumer's behalf. They will protect their margins with their own agents or block outside ones.

## **II.**

On its [Q3 2026 earnings call](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/meta/transcripts/718643-q3-2026/) Meta reported [over 10m Muse setups in the US and Canada with about 25% active daily](https://apnews.com/article/13ecd6c459e39a3602f302bcc2412fe9). Over a quarter of weekly users used it for shopping with average purchases above $200, including travel.

To model three-year business outcomes, let's assume:

- Twice as many weekly users as daily users: **2.5m daily → 5m weekly**.
- A quarter of those weekly users buy once a month at $200: **$3bn in annual purchases**.
- A 5% take for Muse: equivalent to [Stripe's entire processing charge](https://stripe.com/pricing) of roughly 3% plus a 2% convenience fee → **$150m in annual gross fees**.

For a historical benchmark, ChatGPT grew from [more than 100m weekly users in late 2023 to almost 350m in late 2024](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/a253471f-8260-40c6-a2cc-aa93fe9f142e/economic-research-chatgpt-usage-paper.pdf#page=11). Round that to 3.5× in a year, or 250% growth. Repeating that pace for three years would take Muse from 5m to about 214m weekly users: **5m × 3.5³**. The bull case doubles that year-three user count to **429m**; it does not double the annual growth rate.

## **III.**

In year three (so 2029-ish):

- **Bear: 5m → 10m weekly users**, approximately **26% annual growth**. At 25% buying once a month, $200 per purchase and a 5% take: **$300m annual gross fees**.
- **Base: 5m → 50m weekly users**, approximately **115% annual growth**. At 25% buying once a month, $200 per purchase and a 5% take: **$1.5bn annual gross fees**.
- **Bull (2× the ChatGPT projection): 5m → 429m weekly users**, approximately **341% annual growth**. At 50% buying twice a month, $200 per purchase and a 5% take: **$51.5bn annual gross fees**.

The bull case requires roughly 214m shoppers spending $4,800 each a year through Muse: **$1.03tn in gross merchandise value (GMV)**.

That's about 10× [DoorDash's $102bn in 2025 marketplace gross order value](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000179278926000013/dash-20251231.htm). The $4,800 per shopper also exceeds the [$4,280 average annual US ecommerce spend per shopper in Landmark Global's 2026 factsheet](https://landmarkglobal.com/uploads/26-CFS/Factssheet-US-2026.pdf).

At a 5% take and zero costs:

| Annual figures | Muse bull case, year three | [Meta Family of Apps, 2025 actuals](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Revenue | $51.5bn | $198.8bn |
| Operating profit | $51.5bn | $102.5bn |
| Operating margin | 100% assumed | 51.6% actual |

## **IV.**

Compare [Amazon's advertising business](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm) and [Meta's](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm):

| | Revenue / increase |
| --- | ---: |
| Muse bull case, year three (5% total take) | $51.5bn |
| Amazon ad revenue increase, 2025 vs. 2024 | $12.4bn |
| Amazon total ad revenue, 2025 | $68.6bn |
| Meta ad revenue increase, 2025 vs. 2024 | $35.5bn |
| Meta total ad revenue, 2025 | $196.2bn |

Growing Meta's 2025 ad revenue by 26.2% would add $51.5bn a year, matching the Muse bull case. In 2025, ad revenue grew by [22.1%, or $35.5bn](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm). The required growth rate is 4.1 percentage points higher, from a larger base. Putting that compute into better ad targeting feels more sensible.

In a similar vein, on the supply side, Amazon can use agents to improve search and ad targeting in its own store. Advertising revenue can also fund lower prices. If Amazon devoted a quarter of its ad revenue to discounts after covering ad costs, it could cut prices by roughly 2% and swamp an agent's entire convenience fee. An upstart has to pay for compute and customer acquisition out of that fee. Without it, agents are left as a pure processing play, battling giants that have invested trillions in delivery, returns, and the rest of the retail infrastructure, with processing schemes of their own.

## **V.**

Travel is a legitimate consumer use case. Beyond that, b2c shopping agents bet on a consumer preference for productivity that feels absurd when the best consumer product of all time is an infinite feed of nonsense.

The businesses that matter at scale have little reason to support an independent agent serving the shopper. And if refusing to serve shopping agents supposedly makes you a second-tier retailer, the hypothetical mom-and-pop insurgency can look at [Amazon to see who ends up in control](https://www.ft.com/content/64f01bfe-39b9-49f6-86eb-be96374539cf).

In 2026, we're still in search of a broader consumer case.
