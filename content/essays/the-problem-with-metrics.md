Goodhart's Law states that when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure. This is the central tension of running any data-driven organization: the metrics you optimize for inevitably become the metrics you game.

---

Consider customer satisfaction scores. The moment you tie employee bonuses to CSAT, you create an incentive to optimize for the score rather than for actual satisfaction. Agents start asking customers to rate them highly. Difficult cases get escalated rather than resolved. The number goes up while the experience goes down.

The same pattern plays out with engagement metrics, conversion rates, and OKRs of every variety. The measurement infrastructure becomes the thing being managed, and the underlying reality drifts further from view.

---

The solution is not to abandon metrics but to hold them loosely. Use numbers as diagnostic tools rather than as scorecards. Pay attention to what the metrics cannot capture. And periodically ask the uncomfortable question: are we optimizing for the number or for the thing the number was supposed to represent?
