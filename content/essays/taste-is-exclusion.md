The most underrated skill in product development is the willingness to exclude. Every feature request is someone's good idea. The hard part is recognizing that a product with every good idea is a bad product.

---

Taste, in the creative sense, is not about what you include. It is about what you leave out. A well-designed room is defined by the furniture that isn't there. A great essay is shaped by the paragraphs that were cut.

This principle extends beyond aesthetics. The best companies are defined as much by what they refuse to build as by what they ship. Apple famously kills more products than it launches. The result is not a sparse catalog — it is a coherent one.

---

The difficulty is that exclusion feels like loss. Every feature you cut has a constituency. Every simplification removes someone's favorite thing. The organizational gravity of a growing company always pulls toward addition.

Fighting that gravity requires taste, and taste requires confidence — the quiet conviction that less, done well, is more than more, done adequately.
