Technical debt is a well-understood concept. Simplicity debt is not, but it should be. Simplicity debt accumulates every time you add a feature without removing one, every time you introduce a new concept without consolidating existing ones.

---

The cost of simplicity debt is invisible in the short term. Each individual addition seems harmless. But the cumulative effect is a product that requires a manual to understand, an onboarding process to navigate, and a support team to explain.

The most dangerous form of simplicity debt is conceptual. When a product has too many overlapping concepts — tags and labels and categories and folders, each slightly different — users spend more time understanding the system than using it.

---

Paying down simplicity debt requires the same discipline as paying down technical debt: dedicated time, organizational buy-in, and a willingness to remove things that some people use. The difference is that simplicity debt is harder to measure and therefore easier to ignore. By the time it becomes obvious, it is usually too late.
