The remote work debate is usually framed as a productivity question. Can people do their jobs from home? The answer, by now, is obviously yes. But productivity was never the real question.

---

The real question is about loneliness. Office work, for all its inefficiencies, provided a social infrastructure that many people relied on without realizing it. The casual hallway conversation, the lunch with colleagues, the ambient presence of other humans — these were not productivity features. They were social ones.

Remote work removed these interactions and replaced them with nothing. Slack messages are not conversations. Zoom calls are not presence. The result is a generation of knowledge workers who are more productive and more isolated than ever.

---

I don't think the answer is forcing everyone back to the office. The answer is acknowledging that remote work created a social deficit and then deliberately building structures to address it. This means investing in offsites, encouraging local meetups, and recognizing that human connection is not a perk — it is a need.
